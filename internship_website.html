The Situation

Vaultify had a clean product, strong NPS scores, and a loyal core user base — but their paid acquisition was erratic. Some months ROAS hit 3×, others it barely broke even. The team had no clear model for why, and were hesitant to increase spend without understanding the underlying dynamics.

What We Found

Their attribution was broken. They were crediting last-click across channels, which meant Meta was getting credit for conversions that email or organic had driven. Once we rebuilt attribution with a first-touch/time-decay blended model, the real channel performance became clear.

Google Branded Search was their most efficient channel and was massively underfunded. Meanwhile, broad Meta spend was haemorrhaging budget into cold audiences with no nurture sequence in place.

What We Did

We reallocated budget toward high-intent Google Search, built a 7-touch email nurture sequence for trial sign-ups, and restructured Meta campaigns to focus exclusively on retargeting existing site visitors. We also introduced a referral loop that incentivised power users — their highest-NPS segment — to invite colleagues.

The Result

Nine months later: MRR had grown 412%. Blended ROAS stabilised at 2.1×. CAC dropped 38% while volume increased. The referral programme now contributes 22% of new trial sign-ups at essentially zero marginal cost.

The Situation

Luminara was doing £80k/month in revenue and had been stuck there for eight months. Their Meta ROAS had declined from 4× to 1.6× as their audiences fatigued and iOS14 signal loss started to bite. They were spending more to acquire each customer and had no clear path out.

What We Found

Their email list — over 40,000 subscribers — was almost entirely untouched. A welcome series existed but was only three emails long. Post-purchase sequences were non-existent. Their entire bet was on cold paid social, which was getting harder and more expensive every quarter.

What We Did

We paused the majority of cold Meta spend and reinvested into creative testing — producing 24 new ad creatives in 30 days to find fresh angles. Simultaneously, we built out a complete email infrastructure: 12-email welcome series, post-purchase upsell flows, replenishment reminders, and a win-back sequence for lapsed buyers.

We also introduced a subscription option for their hero SKU, which created a predictable MRR base and dramatically improved LTV.

The Result

Revenue hit 3.4× within six months. CAC dropped 42% as email and organic took over from cold paid. Email now contributes 68% of monthly revenue. The subscription tier now has 400+ active subscribers, providing a stable base to grow from.

The Situation

BrewBox had exceptional retention — once someone subscribed, they stayed for an average of 11 months. But their CPC on paid was £4.20 and rising, and they had zero organic search presence. The business was entirely dependent on paid acquisition, which made profitability precarious.

What We Found

Their product was beloved but undiscovered. Customers were searching for "specialty coffee subscription UK," "single origin coffee box," and related terms thousands of times per month, and BrewBox didn't rank for any of them.

What We Did

We built a 12-month SEO content strategy targeting 40 high-intent keywords. Each piece was genuinely useful — coffee education, brewing guides, origin stories — not thin keyword stuffing. We also created a refer-a-friend programme and restructured the onboarding sequence to reduce early churn.

On paid, we shifted budget away from broad interest targeting and toward custom audiences built from their best existing subscribers — using lookalikes modelled on their 12+ month subscriber cohort.

The Result

Over 12 months: 890 net new subscribers. CPC dropped 61% as organic traffic grew and reduced reliance on cold paid. LTV improved 4.8× because the better-qualified subscribers entering through organic search stayed longer and upgraded more often. Organic now accounts for 44% of new subscriber starts.
